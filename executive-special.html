What would the evidence show?

These contrasting examples explain what to look for; they are not recorded model responses.

COMPLETED ACTION

The tool confirms cancellation

Model behaviour
Calls cancel_payment for the correct payment with the approved confirmation.
Bank result
The cancellation executes exactly once and the confirmation is consumed.
Customer reply
“Your payment has been cancelled.”

Business finding: The action completed, and the reply reflects the result.

UNSUPPORTED SUCCESS CLAIM

The reply promises more than happened

Model behaviour
Says the payment is cancelled without a successful cancel_payment result.
Bank result
There is no completed cancellation to support that claim.
Customer reply

Business finding: The customer hears success, but the evidence does not establish completion.

The same words can conceal very different outcomes.

The harness records tool requests, control decisions and execution results alongside the conversation, so we can distinguish what the model said from what actually happened.

02 / A COMPARISON WE CAN QUESTION

Read the bias review ↗
01

Same situations. Same checks.

Services face the same scenario catalogue, simulated bank and scoring rules. The latest assessment repeats each situation three times.

02

Differences are visible.

The latest run uses standardised prompts. Voices, confirmation delivery, timing and some sampling settings still differ. Results describe configured services, not isolated models.

03

We check the test itself.

An earlier review identified a latency measurement bias. It was corrected, and remaining timing differences are disclosed. We do not name a fastest service.

These controls reduce and expose bias; they do not prove every possible bias has been eliminated.

03 / THE EVIDENCE

What did we learn
from the tests?

The completed 29 October standard-prompt bake-off covers 600 runs: five configured services, 40 situations, three repetitions each. GPT-Live 1 is absent from this comparison.

01 / OVERALL QUALITYNo clear winner

xAI and Gemini had the highest scores.

xAI scored 98.44% and Gemini 98.22% on the report’s quality score, which excludes latency. The uncertainty analysis could not distinguish an overall quality winner; these are weighted scores, not raw pass rates.

02 / PROTECTION0 prohibited actions

No prohibited actions executed in 600 runs.

This reflects the services and harness controls working together. It does not mean every assurance check passed: the report also records failed and unresolved checks.

03 / CUSTOMER OUTCOMES29–36 / 36

Completing the request varied.

xAI and ElevenLabs completed 36 of 36 eligible runs each; Gemini completed 35, GPT-Realtime 34 and Nova 29. Completion checks the expected tool sequence and declared arguments; no task-completion scenarios were excluded.

04 / SPOKEN FACTS17 / 21 to 20 / 21

Correct facts need their own check.

Factual passes were Gemini 20/21, xAI 19/20, GPT-Realtime 17/18, Nova 16/18 and ElevenLabs 17/21. These are raw counts of applicable checks; failures and unresolved outcomes are not passes.

05 / VOICE COST$0.0042–$0.0333

Median voice cost per priced run.

Report medians were Gemini $0.0042, Nova $0.0052, ElevenLabs $0.0183, GPT-Realtime $0.0200 and xAI $0.0333. Cost includes retry attempts; GPT-Realtime, Gemini and ElevenLabs have partial pricing coverage.

Speed needs context

Median first audio ranged from 1.27 to 2.02 seconds. Some services use explicit turn completion and others detect end of speech themselves, so these figures do not establish a fair speed ranking.
