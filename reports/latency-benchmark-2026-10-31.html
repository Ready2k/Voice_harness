Latency benchmark

How long a caller waits for the reply, with every service on its own end-of-speech detection. Four sessions on 31 October 2026, 497 timed turns, six providers. Latency only: this is a separate measurement from the quality bake-offs and changes none of their results.

What was measured

Caller-felt latency is the time from the end of the caller's voice to the first reply audio. Where a service reports its own end-of-speech signal it splits into the wait for end of speech (the caller has stopped, the service is still deciding) and the reply start after that signal. ElevenLabs and GPT-Live expose no usable signal, so only the total is shown for them. Gemini's end-of-speech marker was not recorded in the four pooled sessions; a fifth, shorter session (marked †) recorded it, so Gemini's split comes from that session alone and is not pooled into the other figures.

Earlier bake-offs told three services the turn had ended after an emulated 500 ms, so their latency could not be ranked (mixed_turn_control). Here nothing is emulated.

Reading it

  • Plain answers: Google Gemini Live had the lowest median (1.17 s), clear of the others by interval. xAI Grok Voice was slowest (2.07 s).
  • Tool answers: OpenAI Realtime had the lowest median (1.44 s), with OpenAI GPT-Live 1 not separable by interval. ElevenLabs Agents was slowest (2.17 s).
  • Where two intervals overlap the services are not shown to differ. Plain-answer samples are small (20 to 40 turns per service).
  • The wait for end of speech is a large share of the total for the three services that report it, about 0.9 s for Nova Sonic and OpenAI Realtime and about 1.15 s for xAI. It is set by the service's own silence rule, not only by its engine speed.

Method and limits

  • Sessions: four, at 11:19Z, 11:57Z, 14:20Z and 17:20Z. The first held tool scenarios only and 1b held single-turn plain scenarios only; sessions 2 and 3 held both. Tool and plain turns are reported separately.
  • Prompt: standardised-v3 for every provider. Scripted fixed caller voice, trailing synthesiser silence trimmed so the end of speech is the end of the voice.
  • Turn detection: OpenAI Realtime and xAI at 700 ms silence, Gemini at 600 ms, picked from a short calibration pass in which 300 ms and 500 ms cut a deliberate 600 ms hesitation off for OpenAI Realtime and xAI (three trials per setting, rough). Nova, ElevenLabs and GPT-Live ran as shipped.
  • One location and network, with providers running at the same time from one machine. Providers hosted nearby are favoured.
  • Not comparable with earlier bake-off latency: those figures anchored on a marker about 220 ms after the voice stopped. This benchmark anchors on the end of the voice.
  • Turns that overlapped the caller are excluded. Judges and independent speech recognition were off; latency needs neither.
  • Intervals are bootstrap intervals of the median (fixed seed). Turns within a session are not independent, so intervals are optimistic.
  • Data: per-turn results. Rebuild with scripts/build_latency_report.py.
