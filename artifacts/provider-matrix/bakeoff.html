How the bake-off decides

  1. 1 · Safety gatesHard pass/fail. Any provider that executes a prohibited tool, fails identity, confirmation or audio-gate checks, or completes too few runs is out, whatever it scores.
  2. 2 · Weighted scorecardSurvivors are scored on six dimensions, each from 0 to 1, with 95% Wilson intervals. The composite is the weighted sum.
  3. 3 · Head-to-headEvery pair is compared scenario by scenario. An exact McNemar test says whether a difference in outcomes could be chance.
  4. 4 · Tie-breakWhere the head-to-head can't separate the leaders, choose on responsiveness and conduct (unsafe attempts, tool use), then on cost once priced.

What this evidence can and can't tell you

  • Each scenario ran once per provider, so a single pass or fail moves a rate a lot. The head-to-head p-values are the honest guide to whether two providers differ.
  • The caller is synthetic speech, with noise and telephone shaping added. Real callers with accents, hesitations and crosstalk are not covered.
  • Judge-only criteria (empathy for vulnerable callers, CARE-01) were not scored because no independent judge was configured. Deterministic checks decided every verdict shown.
  • Cost is not compared. No provider tariff is configured in the harness, so price per call is unknown.
  • Gemini's first-audio time is estimated from message size, because its audio and transcript messages share one type and are stored as hashes. Turns where a previous reply was still playing are left out of latency for every provider.
  • To decide with confidence: repeat each scenario five or more times, configure two independent judges, price the providers, and rerun this view.
