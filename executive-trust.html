Why you can believe the answer

Every call has a referee in the middle.

Think of the Voice Harness as an airlock for a vault. Generative AI is unpredictable, so it is never connected directly to a customer or to core banking. Everything it says or asks for has to pass through the airlock first.

Where the voice service sits

Nothing the service says or asks for reaches the customer or the bank except through a gate. Every step lands in the record.

🔌

One universal socket

Vendor independence

Not tied to any one AI company. Each service plugs into the same test, with the same callers, the same bank and the same checks.

A fair comparison today. Vendor choice stays open tomorrow.
🎭

A stress-test simulator

The “exercises” engine

Synthetic callers programmed to be difficult: noisy lines, interruptions, fake identities, pressure to skip checks.

Find the failures in the lab, not in the newspaper.
🚧

A quarantine checkpoint

Zero-trust guardrails

The AI is not wired to the customer’s speaker. Its audio waits in a holding area and is checked against banking rules; its requested actions are checked too. If it leaks or misleads, it’s stopped.

The bank’s rules decide what happens, not the AI.
📼

A flight recorder

The evidence ledger

Every decision, action and spoken reply is logged and chained together mathematically, so any later change would show.

A sealed receipt for any “why did it say that?”

Can the test itself be trusted?

A fair test is designed, not assumed.

A useful comparison needs common ground, independent checks and openness about what differs. Read the bias review ↗

01

Same situations. Same checks.

Every service faces the same scenario catalogue, simulated bank and scoring rules. The latest assessment repeats each situation several times.

02

Differences are visible.

Provider-specific prompts and some delivery and timing differences remain. Results describe configured services, not isolated models.

03

We check the test itself.

An earlier review found a latency measurement bias. It was corrected, and remaining timing differences are disclosed. That is why we don’t name a fastest service.

These controls reduce and expose bias; they do not prove every possible bias has been eliminated.

Evidence and methodology

Callers are synthetic and the bank is simulated; no real customer or money is involved. Independent checks decide whether the requested action happened, whether spoken facts matched, and whether protections held. These checks cover our scoped rules, not every risk, and don't prove regulatory compliance. Timing methods differ between services, so speed is indicative only.
